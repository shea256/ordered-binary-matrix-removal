\begin{abstract}

\textbf{Candidate result with a Lean-checked qualitative theorem.} The
forbidden pattern has nine entries, down from twelve in our previous
\(3\times 4\) construction. We retain arbitrary binary repairs,
arbitrarily small distance parameters, and arbitrarily large hosts at
the same chosen parameter. Independent mathematical peer review is
pending; no global minimality or worldwide priority is claimed.

The written construction below has \(n=(8h+2)m\) and exactly \(7m^{4}\)
copies. A separate AI reviewer found no gap in this argument. The Lean
proof establishes the same qualitative theorem using doubled anchor
blocks, \(n=(14h+2)m\), and the sufficient bound \(2mn^{4}\). It does
not certify the written exact count or smaller host size. Section 8
specifies the formal statement and evidence.

\end{abstract}

\section*{Background and relation to prior
work}\label{background-and-relation-to-prior-work}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Background and relation to prior work}

Alon, Fischer and Newman proved polynomial removal bounds for binary
matrices when copies are considered up to row and column permutations
{[}\citeproc{ref-alon-fischer-newman2007}{1}{]}. Alon, Ben-Eliezer and
Fischer established a qualitative removal lemma for ordered matrices and
constant-query testing of hereditary matrix properties
{[}\citeproc{ref-alon-ben-eliezer-fischer2017}{2}{]}. The polynomial
bound of Alon and Ben-Eliezer for hitting all existing copies
{[}\citeproc{ref-alon-ben-eliezer2020}{3}, Theorem 1.2{]} addresses a
different quantity from binary repair distance: editing a hitting set
can create new copies. Here copies preserve the separate row and column
orders, and repairs must eliminate all copies while permitting changes
in either binary direction.

OpenAI gave a fixed \(66\times 66\) obstruction to polynomial ordered
binary matrix removal {[}\citeproc{ref-openai2026}{4}{]}. Subsequent
repository manuscripts include the ProveIt project's \(5\times 5\)
construction {[}\citeproc{ref-proveit2026}{5}{]}, Erebos Labs'
\(4\times 4\) construction {[}\citeproc{ref-erebos2026}{6}{]}, and
Wang's \(4\times 4\) construction with exact copy and repair counts
{[}\citeproc{ref-wang2026}{7}{]}. The present \(3\times 3\) construction
continues our
\href{https://github.com/shea256/ordered-binary-matrix-removal/blob/master/proofs/three-by-four.md}{preceding
\(3\times 4\) refinement}. The bibliography identifies the versions
used;
\href{https://github.com/shea256/ordered-binary-matrix-removal/blob/master/BASELINE.md}{BASELINE.md}
records their distinct proof and formal-verification scopes.

\section{Statement and quantitative
implication}\label{statement-and-quantitative-implication}

Let

\[
 K=J_3-I_3=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\1&0&1\\1&1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For every integer \(h\ge 1\), put \(m=2^h\), \(d=8h+2\) and \(n=dm\).
The square binary host \(A_h\) defined below satisfies

\[
 D_K(A_h)\ge m^2,\qquad N_K(A_h)=7m^4,
 \qquad \frac{N_K(A_h)}{n^6}=7d^{-6}4^{-h}.
\]

\(D_K\) minimizes \textbf{all binary edits in both directions, at
arbitrary cells}. Copies preserve independent increasing row and column
orders and match all nine entries. No exact minimum repair distance is
claimed.

Consequently, for every \(c,C,\epsilon _{0}>0\) there exists a fixed
\(0<\epsilon <\epsilon _{0}\) such that, for every \(N\), some
\(n\ge \max(1,N)\) and binary \(n\times n\) host require at least
\(\epsilon n^{2}\) edits to become \(K\)-free and have fewer than
\(c \epsilon ^C n^{6}\) copies. This is a \(3\times 3\) pattern, with 9
rather than the preceding \(3\times 4\) pattern's 12 entries.

Bitwise complementation gives the same statement for the
\textbf{\boldmath \(3\times 3\) identity matrix}: every \(K\)-copy in
\(A\) corresponds to an \(I_{3}\)-copy in \(1-A\), and \(B\mapsto 1-B\)
preserves edit distance and exchanges the two freeness conditions.

\section{Elementary order and support
facts}\label{elementary-order-and-support-facts}

Blocks in a fixed class occur in increasing node order. A fixed
nonanchor row is nondecreasing along a column class; a fixed nonanchor
column is nonincreasing along a row class. These follow from the four
thresholds. For the comparisons used below, the recursive order gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_{i-1}^+(a)<C_i^\sigma(q)&\iff a\le\pi(q),\\
 C_i^\sigma(q)<C_{i-1}^-(b)&\iff\pi(q)\le b,\\
 C_{i-1}^-(b)<C_i^\sigma(q)&\iff b<\pi(q),\\
 C_j^+(a)<C_j^-(b)&\iff a\le b,\\
 R_i^+(p)<R_{i-1}^+(a)&\iff\pi(p)\le a,\\
 R_{i-1}^-(a)<R_i^-(p)&\iff a\le\pi(p).
\end{aligned}
\]

Child signs can be the common leaf sign; the same-level cross-sign
comparison has \(j<h\). Reverse comparisons have the complementary
inequalities.

\textbf{Nonanchor crown exclusion.} A dummy cannot occur in a crown,
since each crown row and column contains a zero. Two crown columns
cannot be in one variable class, because their one-sets would be nested.
The classes on which a nondummy row can be one are its own and preceding
signed classes, or, for a leaf row, \(L_C,C_{h-1}^+,C_{h-1}^-\). Three
distinct column classes with a common nondummy neighbor for each pair
must therefore be this last triple: elsewhere the class-incidence graph
is two paths. The two crown rows incident to the leaf column must both
be leaves. Their nonanchor neighborhoods are nested as the leaf index
increases, contradicting the crown's incomparable row traces. This
excludes every row/column permutation of the crown in the nonanchor
body.

\textbf{Crossing anchor supports.} Suppose modes \(A<B\) have ordered
variable columns \(u<v<w\) with

\[
 u\in T_B\setminus T_A,\quad v\in T_A\setminus T_B,
 \quad w\in T_A\cap T_B.
\]

Then \(A=W_{i,0}^-\) for \(i<h\), and \(B\) is \(W_{i+1,e}^-\) or
\(V_{i+1}^-\). Moreover \(v\in C_{i-1}^-\), \(w\in C_{i,1}^-\), and
\(u\) is in \(C_{i,0}^-\) or, when \(B=W_{i+1,0}^-\) and \(i+1<h\), in
\(C_{i+1,1}^-\).

Here is an exhaustive justification from the support table. At one minus
level the supports are nested. Distinct minus levels have a variable
intersection only at successive levels with the earlier mode's odd-child
addition. A minus mode can cross a later vertical support only when that
support is its full child class. Plus horizontal supports at distinct
levels have no variable intersection; at one level they are disjoint
apart from \(C_*\). A plus horizontal support and a later vertical
support can cross only in the former's parity class, leaving \(C_*\) as
the first support's exclusive part. It cannot be \(v\), since \(u<v\)
and \(C_*\) is first. Vertical supports are distinct full classes. Plus
and minus variable supports do not intersect: the minus odd-child
addition never reaches the shared leaf class. These exhaust the mode
types and their order.

\section{Every copy has exactly one anchor on each
axis}\label{every-copy-has-exactly-one-anchor-on-each-axis}

Write a copy on \(r_{1}<r_{2}<r_{3}\) and \(c_{1}<c_{2}<c_{3}\). Let
\(a,b\) count its anchor rows and columns. Rows and columns of \(K\) are
pairwise distinct, so no twin block can be used twice. Row anchors form
a prefix of the copy; column anchors form a suffix.

\textbf{\boldmath \(a=0\).} If \(b=0\), use nonanchor crown exclusion.
Otherwise \(c_{3}=Y_t\). Its ones on \(r_{1},r_{2}\) put them in the
same variable class: neither can be the last nonanchor row type, the
dummy. If \(b=1\) or 2, \(c_{1}\) is nonanchor, and its trace \(01\) on
\(r_{1}<r_{2}\) contradicts nonincreasing entries within their row
class. If \(b=3\), \(c_{1}\) and \(c_{2}\) have opposite traces \(01\)
and \(10\) on those two rows. On one variable class a \(U\) support is
empty, full, or one parity half. Thus these two supports must be the two
opposite parity halves, both belonging to horizontal modes. They are
disjoint even on the dummy, contradicting their two ones on \(r_{3}\).

\textbf{\boldmath \(a\ge 1, b\ge 2\).} Row \(r_{1}\) is an \(X\) block
and is one on both \(c_{2},c_{3}\). It is one on only one \(Y\) block,
so these columns would be twins.

\textbf{\boldmath \(a\ge 2, b=1\).} Column \(c_{3}\) is one on both
\(r_{1},r_{2}\). Its unique matching \(X\) block makes these rows twins.

\textbf{\boldmath \(a\ge 2, b=0\).} Put \((u,v,w)=(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})\).
The common \(w\) and the middle \(v\) cannot be dummy because earlier
columns exist. If \(u\) were dummy, the earlier anchor would be vertical
and the later one horizontal, contrary to the global mode order. Thus
all three columns are variable, and the first two supports give the
crossing configuration of Section 3. If \(a=2\), \(r_{3}\) is nondummy
(it is zero on \(w\)). It must be one on \(u,v\). When \(u\) is at level
\(i+1\), the two levels \(i+1\) and \(i-1\) are too far apart for one
nonanchor row support. When \(u\) is in even \(C_i^-\), \(r_{3}\) must
be in \(R_i^-\). Since \(u<w\) within that class, its one on \(u\)
forces a one on \(w\), a contradiction. If \(a=3\), no anchor support
contains both \(C_{i-1}^-\) and \(C_{i+1}^-\). The only anchor support
spanning \(C_{i-1}^-\) and \(C_i^-\) adds odd children, not the required
even \(u\). This is also impossible.

We are left with \(a=1\) and \(b\le 1\). Suppose \(b=0\). Now
\(c_{2},c_{3}\) lie in \(T_t\) for \(r_{1}=X_t\). They are variable,
since a smaller \(c_{1}\) exists. If they belong to the same class, the
trace \(10\) of \(r_{3}\) on \(c_{2}<c_{3}\) is impossible. The only
remaining support is \(T_{W_{i,0}^-}\), \(i<h\), and their classes are
\(C_{i-1}^-,C_i^-\) in some order. The common positive column \(c_{1}\)
of \(r_{2},r_{3}\) is not dummy, since this \(T_t\) contains \(C_*\).
Both lower rows are nondummy. Inspection of their own/parent supports
puts the class of \(c_{1}\) in \{\(C_{i-1}^-,C_i^-\)\}: a row incident
to the former has depth \(i-1\) or \(i\), and a row incident to the
latter has depth \(i\) or \(i+1\); intersections have no third class,
including when \(i+1=h\). If \(c_{1}\) has \(c_{2}\)'s class, row
\(r_{2}\) has forbidden trace \(10\) on \(c_{1}<c_{2}\). If it has
\(c_{3}\)'s class, row \(r_{3}\) has that trace on \(c_{1}<c_{3}\).
Therefore \(b=1\).

\section{Localization and exact
enumeration}\label{localization-and-exact-enumeration}

We now have \(r_{1}=X_t\) and \(c_{3}=Y_t\), with the same mode because
their corner entry is one. Row \(r_{2}\) is variable, lies in row role 1
of \(t\), and satisfies

\[
 c_1\notin T_t,\quad c_2\in T_t,\quad
 A(r_2,c_1)=1,\quad A(r_2,c_2)=0.
\]

The later row \(r_{3}\) is one on both nonanchor columns and zero on
\(Y_t\).

\textbf{\boldmath \(t=V_i^+\).} Here \(r_{2}=R_i^+(p)\),
\(c_{2}=C_{i-1}^+(q)\), and \(\pi (p)>q\). Since \(r_{3}\) is zero on
\(Y_t\), it is neither dummy nor in \(R_i^+\). Its one on \(c_{2}\)
forces \(r_{3}=R_{i-1}^+(a)\). Row order gives \(\pi (p)\le a\), while
that one gives \(a\le q\), a contradiction.

\textbf{\boldmath \(t=V_i^-\).} Now \(r_{2}=R_{i-1}^-(p)\),
\(c_{2}=C_{i-1}^-(q)\), \(p\ge q\). The zero on \(Y_t\) excludes the
dummy and \(R_{i-1}^-\), so \(r_{3}\) must be \(R_i^-(a)\), allowing a
leaf. Row order and its one on \(c_{2}\) give \(p\le \pi (a)<q\), a
contradiction. These two exclusions use the new dummy ones on vertical
\(Y\) blocks.

\textbf{\boldmath \(t=W_{i,e}^+\).} The row \(r_{2}\) and column
\(c_{2}\) are in the same plus child class and parity; their nodes
\(p,q\) satisfy \(p>q\). A positive \(c_{1}\) outside \(T_t\) cannot be
dummy. If \(c_{1}\) is in the child class, monotonicity contradicts
\(c_{1}<c_{2}\). If it is a plus parent with node \(u\), positivity and
order give \(\pi (p)\le u\le \pi (q)\), impossible for \(p>q\) of equal
parity. At the leaf level a minus parent is additionally possible, but
then \(\pi (p)<u<\pi (q)\), also impossible.

\textbf{\boldmath \(t=W_{i,e}^-\).} Write \(r_{2}=R_i^-(p)\), or
\(L_R(p)\) when \(i=h\). If \(c_{2}\) is an added odd child, then
\(i<h\), \(e=0\), and its node \(q\) satisfies \(p\ge q\). The only
positive \(c_{1}\) outside \(T_t\) is an own-level node \(u>p\); its
order before \(c_{2}\) gives \(u<q\), impossible. Thus
\(c_{2}=C_{i-1}^-(b)\) and \(\pi (p)\ge b\).

For \(i<h\), a positive \(c_{1}\) outside \(T_t\) must be \(C_i^-(q)\),
with \(q>p\). Its position before \(c_{2}\) gives \(\pi (q)\le b\). Thus
\(\pi (q)=b=\pi (p)\), \(p\) is even, and \(q=p+1\) is odd. But this odd
child belongs to \(T_t\), a contradiction. This is the precise use of
the nonterminal odd-child addition.

Consequently \(i=h\). Column \(c_{1}\) is either a leaf \(q\ge p\) or a
plus-parent \(C_{h-1}^+(u)\) with \(\pi (p)\le u\). Its order before
\(c_{2}\) gives \(\pi (q)\le b\) in the first case and \(u\le b\) in the
second. Combining with \(\pi (p)\ge b\) forces

\[
 b=\pi(p),\qquad
 c_1\in\{L_C(q):p\le q,\ \pi(q)=\pi(p)\}
          \ \cup\ C_{h-1}^+(\pi(p)).
\]

Finally \(r_{3}\) must be dummy. A variable row one on
\(C_{h-1}^-(\pi (p))\) is either a leaf \(v\) or \(R_{h-1}^-(a)\). A
later leaf has \(v>p\) and cannot satisfy \(\pi (v)<\pi (p)\). A later
parent-minus row has \(a>\pi (p)\) and cannot satisfy \(a<\pi (p)\). No
other variable row can be one there.

We have classified every copy: for a leaf \(p\), it uses
\(X_{W_{h,p \bmod 2}^-}\), \(L_R(p)\), \(R_*\); \(C_{h-1}^-(\pi (p))\);
the matching \(Y\) block; and either a leaf \(q\ge p\) in that parent's
two-leaf interval or \(C_{h-1}^+(\pi (p))\) as its first column.
Conversely these choices have the required order and all nine entries,
directly by the host rules.

Three anchor/dummy choices contribute \(m^{3}\), and the minus-parent
column contributes 2. The first column has 2 plus-parent choices and 2
leaf choices for even \(p\), or 2 plus-parent choices and 1 leaf choice
for odd \(p\). There are \(m/2\) leaves of each parity, so

\[
 N_K=\frac m2\cdot2m^3(4+3)=7m^4.
\]

The two families contribute \(4m^{4}\) and \(3m^{4}\) respectively. In
particular every copy's middle zero lies in the \(2m\)-cell
leaf-to-own-minus-parent set.

\section{Lower bound against arbitrary
repairs}\label{lower-bound-against-arbitrary-repairs}

Let a binary \(B\) differ from \(A_h\) on \(M<m^{2}\) cells. Sample a
uniform leaf \(z\) and its ancestor path. Independently choose a uniform
representative from each signed row and column block on the path,
identifying the two sign choices at the shared leaf on each axis. Choose
independent uniform representatives from every anchor and dummy block.

Every variable or dummy position has marginal probability \(1/m\), and
each anchor position has probability \(1/m\). A cell with an anchor or
dummy endpoint has selection probability \(1/m^{2}\), since that
representative is independent of the other axis. The same holds for
either root-to-root cell. Protect all such selected cells. The union
bound gives a selection with no edited protected cell. We make no such
probability assertion for general variable-variable cells or the shared
leaf diagonal.

If \(B\) is \(K\)-free, every selected ordered role body must avoid
\(P\), by the unchanged five anchor incidences completing it to \(K\).
The root-plus diagonal is one and root-minus diagonal zero. Assuming
these values at a parent, the two vertical bodies are

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&b_+\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&b_-\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\(P\)-freeness forces \(b_+=1,b_-=0\). The horizontal bodies of the
selected child parity are then

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&c_+\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}c_-&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\]

forcing \(c_+=1,c_-=0\). All four bodies are ordered by the recursive
tree order and have their dummy entries protected. Induction reaches the
common leaf diagonal, a contradiction. Therefore \(D_K(A_h)\ge m^{2}\).
Edits to anchors, dummies and either binary label were never restricted.

\section{Final quantifiers and
blowups}\label{final-quantifiers-and-blowups}

Set \(\epsilon _h=(8h+2)^{-2}\). For every fixed \(C>0\),

\[
 \frac{N_K(A_h)}{n_h^6\epsilon_h^C}
 =7(8h+2)^{2C-6}4^{-h}\longrightarrow0.
\]

Given \(c,C,\epsilon _{0}>0\), choose \(h\) with
\(\epsilon _h<\epsilon _{0}\) and this ratio\({}<c\). Replace each
physical row and column by \(q\) consecutive identical twins. The
pattern's distinct rows and columns prevent a copy from reusing a
projected row or column, so its count multiplies exactly by \(q^{6}\).
The repair sampler works with blocks \(q\) times larger: protected-cell
probabilities are \((qm)^{-2}\), and the lower bound is \(q^{2}m^{2}\).
Both normalized bounds are unchanged. Arbitrarily large \(q\) yields
every prescribed lower threshold on host size at the \textbf{same}
\(\epsilon _h\).

\href{https://github.com/shea256/ordered-binary-matrix-removal/blob/master/formal/OrderedRemoval/Crown/Complete.lean}{Complete.lean}.
It proves the qualitative theorem of Section 1 with the same literal
\(3\times 3\) crown and \(n^{6}\) normalization: every
\(c,C,\epsilon _{0}>0\) admits a fixed \(0<\epsilon <\epsilon _{0}\)
such that every host-size threshold \(N\) admits an \(n\ge \max(1,N)\)
and a square binary host with at least \(\epsilon n^{2}\) edits required
in every crown-free binary repair and fewer than \(c \epsilon ^C n^{6}\)
ordered copies. There are no host-existence, localization, sampling or
anchor-preservation hypotheses.

For this certificate each anchor block on each axis is doubled to size
\(2m\), represented by two consecutive identical subgroups of size
\(m\). All body and dummy blocks, entry rules and relative block orders
are retained. The formal host has \(n=(14h+2)m\). Localization bounds
the number of eligible middle-zero cells by \(2m\); encoding the other
four indices gives at most \(2mn^{4}\) copies. The protected sampler
still gives a repair lower bound \(m^{2}\). Consequently
\(\epsilon =(14h+2)^{-2}\) and the density is at most
\(2\epsilon 2^{-h}\). Exponential decay and uniform blowups prove the
full quantifier order. This certifies the qualitative \(3\times 3\)
result, not the exact \(7m^{4}\) count on the more economical written
host.

\href{https://github.com/shea256/ordered-binary-matrix-removal/blob/master/verification/reference/crown-formal-audit.json}{source-bound
audit} built and checked 92 local modules, 573 named theorems and 2,163
declarations, including inherited infrastructure. The Crown development
adds 19 modules, 88 named theorems and 257 declarations. Only
\nolinkurl{propext}, \nolinkurl{Quot.sound}, and

module was replayed into its imported Lean environment; the audit does
not freshly replay all Mathlib or use a different proof assistant. Lean

\nolinkurl{d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612}.

check} compares the actual Lean host and axis orders with a separate
